# R1-05 Learning critique

**Strongest.** Role 7's checker contract: a separate `unparsed` reply, and rejections substituting the student's own root into the original.

**Most dangerous.** Role 10's moderated localhost gate. Six friends at the owner's laptop finish politely, some jumping straight to x=3. It passes, months of M2-M5 follow, then strangers quit at home.

**Conflicts.**
- Stop rule: keep my metric, eight codes and 5-of-8 line. Role 10 passes 5/6, stops under 4, leaves 4 undefined. Computed: true 80% passes his 66%, mine 94%; true 30%, his 1%, mine 6%. Adopt his cannot/will-not classification: failures that are all cannot get one input fix and retest; any will-not majority stops. Localhost-only sessions can stop, never certify willingness.
- Strings: role 9 wins. Rejected input is exactly what escapes the allowlist. Export token shapes, adding unknown-character class (× versus *). Observers note the rest.
- Root leak: only an empty-solution line, like `0=1`, forces showing the answer. Say "this line has no solution; the original does". The witness appears on "Show why", counted as bottom-out.

**Retract.** Raw rejected strings. Always-shown counterexamples. Keeping P0n.txt: transcribe per-code results, then delete messages.
